# Mathematical Foundations of the Topological Field-Adaptive Network

The following six theorems summarise the formal analysis of the Topological
Field-Adaptive Network (T-FAN). Proof sketches emphasise the constructive
arguments implemented in the accompanying code. Complete derivations with
supporting lemmas are maintained in the internal Quanta lab notebooks.

## Theorem 1 — Stability of the Differentiable Persistence Encoder

*Statement.* Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a compact metric space and let $D(\mathcal{X})$
be the persistence diagram generated by a $1$-Lipschitz filtration. The
persistence encoder $\Phi_\theta$ used in T-FAN is $C \cdot \varepsilon$-stable
with respect to perturbations of magnitude $\varepsilon$ in the bottleneck
metric.

*Proof.* The encoder in both frameworks is a composition of linear layers,
LayerNorm, GELU, and the sigmoid map. Each component is Lipschitz continuous,
with constants $L_i$. By the stability of persistence diagrams, the bottleneck
distance between $D(\mathcal{X})$ and $D(\mathcal{Y})$ is bounded by $\varepsilon$
for filtrations perturbed by $\varepsilon$. Therefore,
$\|\Phi_\theta(D(\mathcal{X})) - \Phi_\theta(D(\mathcal{Y}))\| \leq (\prod_i L_i)
\varepsilon$, giving the claimed bound with $C = \prod_i L_i$.

## Theorem 2 — Approximation of Laplace-Beltrami Kernels

*Statement.* Consider smooth vector fields $f : \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ on
a compact Riemannian manifold $\mathcal{M}$. The discrete field kernel used in
T-FAN converges uniformly to the Laplace-Beltrami operator as the sampling
density of the point cloud tends to infinity.

*Proof.* The kernel aggregates pairwise interactions via a normalised Gram
matrix. By the convergence guarantees of Belkin & Niyogi (2007), the graph
Laplacian computed from dense samples converges to the Laplace-Beltrami
operator. Our kernel applies a linear projection followed by LayerNorm, which is
continuous, so uniform convergence is preserved by composition.

## Theorem 3 — Expressivity of Adaptive Routing

*Statement.* The stack of topological blocks can approximate any continuous map
$f : \mathbb{S}^{d-1} \to \mathbb{R}^k$ that is piecewise smooth with respect to
a finite stratification of $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$.

*Proof.* Each block mixes local linear projections with a multiplicative gate
conditioned on the persistence summaries. The gate outputs lie in $(0, 1)^d$,
realising a partition of unity over the stratification. By the universal
approximation theorem, the feedforward layers approximate each smooth piece,
and the gating ensures they interpolate continuously along boundaries. Stacking
blocks increases the number of regions exponentially, ensuring coverage of the
finite stratification.

## Theorem 4 — Convergence of the Adaptive AdamW Schedule

*Statement.* With a step size satisfying $\sum_t \eta_t = \infty$ and
$\sum_t \eta_t^2 < \infty$, the AdamW optimiser with the adaptive learning rate
schedule specified in the reference implementation converges to a stationary
point of the empirical risk.

*Proof.* The adaptive schedule rescales $\eta_t$ by the moving average of the
TopologicalFieldAdaptiveNetwork's gradient norms. By Lemma 1 of Reddi et al.
(2018), AdamW converges under bounded gradients and appropriately decaying step
sizes. The gradient clipping routine in the scripts enforces boundedness,
allowing the lemma to be applied directly.

## Theorem 5 — Robustness to Vanishing Topological Features

*Statement.* Suppose the persistence diagram collapses to the diagonal. Then the
attention weights in each block converge to the field-kernel-only regime, and
the network reduces to a stable residual MLP.

*Proof.* When persistence points vanish, the encoded gate tends to $\sigma(0) =
1/2$. By symmetry, the residual branch degenerates to averaging the field kernel
and the latent projection, which is equivalent to a residual MLP with constant
scaling. Since residual MLPs are stable under bounded perturbations, the result
follows.

## Theorem 6 — Cross-Framework Equivalence

*Statement.* For matching seeds and identical hyperparameters, the JAX and
PyTorch implementations produce outputs whose relative difference is bounded by
machine epsilon after one forward pass on the same inputs.

*Proof.* Both code paths use Glorot initialisation, GELU activations, and layer
normalisation with identical $\epsilon$. The only discrepancies arise from the
backend BLAS libraries, leading to floating point differences of order machine
epsilon. Therefore $\|f_{\text{JAX}}(x) - f_{\text{PyTorch}}(x)\| / \|x\|
\leq \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{\text{mach}})$.
